The Secret Library

2026 · Design and build, solo

What it is

A personal knowledge library for design work. One JSON file is rendered as five views: a shelf of categorised cards, a graph of the links, a skill tree, a paged book and a sortable index.

The brief

The brief was my own. I wanted one place to collect what I learn about design, and I wanted to see how far one data file and plain JavaScript would go. The constraints were fixed from the start: no framework, no build step, no backend. Everything on the page has to be derived from that one file.

What I did

One file, five views

Adding an entry means adding one object to the file. The sidebar, counts, filters, tag suggestions and the graph are all derived at load. The alternative was several files or a database, but then writing costs more than skipping it, and a library nobody writes in dies.

The added and tended fields

added says when an entry was written. tended says when I last read it again and confirmed it still holds. I never backfill tended. An entry without it is honest, not an oversight.

The weight field

Every entry has a weight from 1 to 5, set by hand. It measures how much I actually use the entry, not how good it is. The shelf's default sort uses weight first. Ties are broken by whether the entry has body text, then status, then link count.

Links with direction

A new entry lists its own links, and older entries are never edited. Backlinks are derived at load, and mutual links are shown only once.

Small screens

Below 767 pixels the graph and the tree are too cramped to use, so the index takes over. The swap does not change ?view= in the address. A link shared from a phone still opens the graph on a wider screen.

Status without colour alone

Seedling has a dashed outline, growing a half-toned fill, evergreen a filled node. The four highlight marks each have their own glyph on top of their own colour.

Motion that can be declined

Under prefers-reduced-motion the graph renders already settled, the book swaps pages without rotating, and the tree stops glowing. The graph physics pause when the tab is hidden or the graph is off screen.

Validation on every load

The data file is checked every time the page opens. Duplicate ids, broken links, illegal values and bad dates print as a console.table, and ?dev in the address shows a visible banner.

What I would change

Five views was one too many. The brain and the tree were worth building, but they do not get used. I rarely open them myself, and they do nothing the shelf and the index do better.

The brain stays. Links between entries are the one thing none of the other views show, and the problem is in how it is built, not in the idea.

The tree goes. The subject areas it shows are already in the shelf's categories, and it gives no new way into the data. What takes its place, I have not decided.

The statuses do not do what they are meant to. Seedling, growing and evergreen are supposed to say how much I trust an entry. In practice they say when I added it. Everything I imported in July sits at growing. The two longest entries in the library, both with their own diagrams and sources, sit at seedling, while a one-line link to Google Fonts sits at evergreen.

The design tools category holds two different things. Figma and Penpot are tools. unDraw, Open Peeps and Openverse are sources of assets. They end up on the same shelf because I did not decide when I added them.

What replaces the tree, I think, has to be a view of tending. The library is built on the idea of a garden where entries are looked after, but none of the views shows what is old or unfinished. That is the one dimension the data has and the views do not use.
