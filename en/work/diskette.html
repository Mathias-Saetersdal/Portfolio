Diskette

2026 · Design and build, solo

What it is

Four lists of ten entries: films, series, albums, games. Each entry appears as the object it was released as. A DVD case, a Blu-ray case, a CD cover, a game case. The case opens on its hinge, the disc lifts out of the tray and spins.

The brief

The brief was my own. Letterboxd, Backloggd and Topsters already handle ranking, and all of them show the same flat grid of cover art. I wanted to see if the object itself could carry the list. The constraints were fixed from the start: frontend only, no backend, no database. The lists live in a TypeScript file I edit by hand. Every image is fetched once, processed and committed to the repo. CSS transforms before WebGL. Anything that works with a mouse has to work with touch.

What I did

Case type follows release year

A film I know from DVD and a film I know from Blu-ray are two different objects. I chose the format I actually owned, not the newest release. Films from 2008 onward get Blu-ray, older films get DVD. The year is an approximation, but it matches my shelf better than a rule based on the canonical release.

Three sources for the disc

Every entry resolves its disc in the same order. First, a scan of the actual disc. Then a generated disc: the cover is cropped to a circle, the hole and the clear inner ring are punched out, and a sheen is laid over it. Last, a burned disc. That last option is also a choice I can set by hand. The Life of Pablo, Rocket League and Cuphead never existed on a disc I owned, and there a burned CD-R is more accurate than a scan of something else.

The burned disc

A silver base, rainbow diffraction, a printed ring near the hub in small type, and the title written across it in Permanent Marker. The disc sits two to five degrees off and slightly off-centre. A perfectly clean blank disc looks like a rendering error. A blank disc with a ring of illegible small print looks like something from a spindle.

The hinge, and the spine that wasn't there

The case opens with a single rotation on the Y axis, on an element with preserve-3d, with the pivot on the spine edge. The first version produced a visible spine that wasn't in the model. I measured six corner coordinates before touching the code, and the error was in transform-origin: the pivot sat in the middle of the face instead of on the edge, so the wall got drawn where the spine should have been. The cause sits as a comment in the file, along with the state before the fix.

Millimetres in the code

The measurements are real millimetres used as ratios. The Blu-ray case is 148 × 128.5 × 12 mm. I had 171 × 135 in the spec at first, from a source I hadn't checked against a real case. Every number carries a comment on where it comes from, so the next version of me doesn't reverse the correction.

Livery per console generation

PS2, PS3, PS4 and PS5 share geometry but not appearance. Each generation has its own header bar, colour and logo placement. Sly 3 gets the black PS2 bar, Dark Souls III the blue PS4 bar, Elden Ring Nightreign the white PS5 bar. Without this every game reads as the same case, and that's wrong for all four.

The images are fetched once

A script fetches covers and disc scans, normalises them to a fixed size and centre, and writes them to the repo. Nothing is fetched from an API while the site runs. The script uses curl instead of fetch, because archive.org's CDN answers unreliably to the latter, and a --only flag lets me re-run a single entry without burning through the quota on the nineteen that already worked. The sources are TMDB, fanart.tv, MusicBrainz with the Cover Art Archive, and GameTDB. All of them require credit. Every file gets a row in credits.json with its source and terms, and that list is shown on the site.

Motion that can be turned off

With prefers-reduced-motion the disc stops spinning and the hinge switches state without rotating. Keyboard focus is visible, and every case and every disc can be reached with tab.

The note

Every entry has two sentences on why it's there, written by me. Forty objects with no text is a tech demo. Forty objects with two honest sentences each is a personal site.

What it looks like

The starting point is a shelf of closed cases shown as spines in a row: blue for Blu-ray, black for DVD, a narrow bare edge for the game cases. A row unfolds to full front covers on touch, with real cover art for the films and albums and generated disc art for the games that never had a retail scan.

The four PlayStation liveries read as distinct even at this size. PS2 has a black bar with white text. PS3 has a grey-to-black gradient with a thin red rule under it. PS4 has a blue bar. PS5 has the only light bar of the four, white. Sly 3 and Lego Star Wars: The Complete Saga also use real retail cover art instead of generated art, visible as a different print sharpness from the rest of the games row.

What I would change

Keyboard doesn't open a case yet. With a case focused, neither Enter nor space did anything in my testing, only an actual mouse click unfolded the row. That breaks the rule I set myself, that anything that works with a mouse has to work with touch and keyboard, and it's the first thing I'd fix.

The button's accessible name doesn't change. It reads Open case: title regardless of state, so a screen reader gets no confirmation that anything actually opened, even when the mouse click works.

The hinge and the disc described above exist as their own component, but the list currently uses a simpler flat case that just enlarges in place. Wiring the full mechanism into all forty entries without killing performance on a mid-range phone is the work that's left.
